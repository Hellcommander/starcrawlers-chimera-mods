[size=5][b]Description[/b][/size]

This is a large expansion for [b]StarCrawlers Chimera[/b], built to give every run more to play with. It adds [b]58 new classes[/b] to mix into your 7-tree build. Each is a full skill tree with its own abilities, stack mechanic and art. None of them borrow vanilla abilities, so you can combine them freely with each other and with the vanilla trees.

Around the classes, it brings back what the first StarCrawlers had and Chimera left out:
[list]
[*][b]Side areas:[/b] breaches on Chimera's floors lead to freshly generated places with anomalies, objectives and sealed vaults.
[*][b]Factions:[/b] five factions from outside the building hire you for jobs, track your reputation and come after you when you cross them.
[*][b]New enemies:[/b] ten of them, each with its own skills, model and animations.
[*][b]Crew:[/b] hireable party members who wear the gear you give them and take orders.
[/list]

Beating Cerberus no longer has to end the run: the credits roll in game, and then you can stay and descend into [b]the Ichor Deep[/b], an endless side area that gets harder and richer with every depth.

On top of that come [b]skill reshaping[/b], where passives, stances and runes rewrite your other skills, [b]247 class-interaction synergies[/b], a crafting window, a class search box, custom character pictures and portraits of your character, hotbar and stash pages, a death recap and Graveyard, diagonal movement, a third-person dash camera, an in-game Mod Settings page, an optional rebalance of the Rogue Modder and more than twenty fixes to bugs in the base game.

Everything runs on the [b]Breachwork Loader[/b], a BepInEx plugin. Each part of the pack is a separate mod that you can switch on or off, down to single classes, items and synergies. The loader is also a full modding framework: new classes, items, enemies, factions and side areas are written in plain JSON or XML with no code, and mods can go further with sandboxed Lua scripts or C# plugins.

[size=5][b]Installation instructions[/b][/size]

This is a manual install: the archive is laid out like the game folder.

[b]Fresh install[/b]
[list=1]
[*]Find the game folder: in Steam, right-click [b]StarCrawlers Chimera > Manage > Browse local files[/b]. It is the folder that holds [i]StarCrawlers Chimera.exe[/i].
[*]Extract everything in the [b]Breachwork[/b] .7z archive into that folder (with 7-Zip, or Windows 11's built-in Extract All) and let it merge the folders. You should end up with [i]BepInEx\[/i], [i]Mods\[/i], [i]winhttp.dll[/i], [i]doorstop_config.ini[/i] and [i]BreachworkModManager.exe[/i] next to the game's .exe.
[*]Start the game. The first start takes a little longer while BepInEx sets itself up.
[*]Start a new character. The new classes are in their archetype columns (Muscle, Guile, Intellect) on the [b]Select Your Skills[/b] screen. When there are more than four rows, the grid scrolls with the mouse wheel, the scrollbar or a controller.
[/list]

[b]Updating[/b]
Extract the new archive over the old one and let it overwrite. Your mod choices, load order and settings are kept. Pages 2 and up of your stash are kept too, because they're stored with your saves, not in the game folder. An old [i]ChimeraModManager.exe[/i] from an earlier release is removed for you on the next start.

[b]Turning mods on and off[/b]
[list]
[*][b]In game:[/b] [b]Mods[/b] on the title screen lists every mod with an On/Off switch. Selecting one shows its description, what it needs and which of your saves use its classes. Changes apply after a restart; [b]Restart Now[/b] does it for you.
[*][b]Outside the game:[/b] run [i]BreachworkModManager.exe[/i] while the game is closed. Tick mods on or off, drag them to change the load order, and install other people's mods from a .zip or a folder. [b]Play[/b] starts the game.
[*][b]Single entries:[/b] [b]Options > Mod Settings > Mod Content[/b] lets you turn off one class, item, synergy, faction or side area kind without turning off the whole mod.
[/list]

[b]Settings[/b]
Every setting is in [b]Options > Mod Settings[/b], on the title screen and in game, with toggles, number steppers, hotkey binding and a reset button. They are also saved in [i]BepInEx\config\arendeth.starcrawlers.classloader.cfg[/i].

[b]Uninstalling[/b]
[list]
[*]To start the game without mods, delete [i]winhttp.dll[/i] and [i]doorstop_config.ini[/i] from the game folder.
[*]To remove everything, also delete the [i]BepInEx[/i] and [i]Mods[/i] folders and [i]BreachworkModManager.exe[/i].
[*]Leave any side area before you uninstall, because a save made inside one needs the mod to load.
[/list]

[size=4][b]1. 58 new classes[/b][/size]

Every class has up to 9 tiers (vanilla trees have 7; the skills screen gets the extra slots). Pick 7 trees as usual: a new class can sit next to any vanilla tree or any other new class.

[size=4][b]2. Skill reshaping[/b][/size]

Passives, stances, one-shot charges and socketed runes can rewrite other skills. A skill can change its delivery (trap, grenade, walking bomb, drifter, nova, line, cone, area, chain or scatter), its element, its damage type, its effects and its behavior, and the tooltip and aiming follow. It keeps its own scaling.
[list]
[*][b]Spellsmith[/b] is the class built around this: an example class, included, that beats the attacks you learned from your other classes into grenades, buried charges and walking bombs. Hammer Form fires an attack through your weapon, with its animation, effects and ammo: the attack keeps its own damage and takes the weapon's reach, accuracy and on-hit effects. Take it beside another class with mod attacks; its forms don't touch vanilla skills.
[*][b]Rune matrix augments:[/b] rune casters such as Flux Savant spend a shared pool on delivery and behavior changes, and take flaws that give points back.
[/list]

[size=4][b]3. Breaches and Echoes: side areas[/b][/size]

Each new floor has a 60% chance of a purple [b]breach pad[/b] somewhere off the main rooms. Stepping on it takes you out of Chimera into a place generated for that breach, with its own layout, lighting and fog. Enemies and loot match your current floor, and the room you arrive in is always clear.

[spoiler]
[b]Places:[/b] Derelict Hauler, Mercenary Outpost, Research Annex and Ore Rock, each with its own layout.

[b]Kinds of side area[/b], shown by the pad's colour:
[list]
[*][b]Derelict:[/b] the crew is gone and something moved in. Always infested and dark, with big caches.
[*][b]Server Farm:[/b] plant three uplinks by standing on each for three turns; every uplink brings responders.
[*][b]Siege:[/b] light the beacon and survive three waves.
[*][b]Marked Hideout:[/b] a much tougher named target with bodyguards, which drops its kit.
[*][b]Grey Market:[/b] a vendor bot selling rare and epic gear.
[*][b]Rift:[/b] goes deeper, up to three layers, each worse and with a better vault.
[/list]

[b]Anomalies:[/b] each breach rolls one to three, such as Blackout, Gas Leak, Lockdown, Infestation, Collapsing Echo (50 turns to get out), Static Storm, Reinforced Garrison, Rich Seam, Alarm Grid, Stalker, Sentry Grid and Stim Haze. A rare Reliquary raises the vault two grades.

[b]The vault:[/b] the room furthest from where you arrive is sealed, and a warden outside carries the override. Vaults go from grade 1 to 5: harder visits give better loot, and grade 5 holds a guaranteed legendary.

[b]Who's inside:[/b] themed enemy rosters (CorpSec Detail, Rogue Machines, Scrap Pirates, Escaped Specimens and more), elites with affixes (Armored, Frenzied, Hulking, Shrouded, Mending), and named rival crawlers that drop epics.

[b]Coming back:[/b] the breach stays open. Leave and step on it again later, and you're back on the layer you left, as you left it: the dead stay dead, opened caches stay open, and the vault and objective are where you got them to. Only a Collapsing Echo running out with you inside closes a breach for good.

[b]Searching:[/b] crates, lockers and cabinets are real containers. A handful in each place hold something, some locked and, deeper down, maybe a mimic; the rest are already picked clean.

[b]Saving:[/b] you can save and quit inside, and the same layout comes back. The Apartment portal works there too. Chimera's own floors are unchanged apart from the pad itself. A brightness setting turns generated places up or down if you find them too dark.
[/spoiler]

[size=4][b]4. After the ending: credits and the Ichor Deep[/b][/size]

Beat Cerberus and the credits roll right there, the game's own and then the mods'. Then you choose: [b]return to the title[/b] as in vanilla, or [b]stay and descend[/b]. The arena's locks come off, the Apartment Portal works again, and a green breach opens beside you into [b]the Ichor Deep[/b].

[spoiler]
[list]
[*][b]Endless:[/b] each layer is a new generated place or an echo of any floor, with its own roster and anomalies, and a rift one depth down. Start at depth 1 or resume at the deepest depth you've reached.
[*][b]Harder:[/b] depth 1 is as dangerous as the final floor. Every depth adds 15% to enemy stats and 20% to their health, raises their level and XP, and makes elites more common. One more hard anomaly joins every third depth.
[*][b]Richer:[/b] caches hold more items the deeper you go, Epic from depth 3 and Legendary from depth 6, and vaults gain a grade every two depths.
[*][b]Scored:[/b] your deepest depth counts toward your Graveyard score.
[*][b]Optional:[/b] the per-depth percentages can be changed, and both the in-game credits and the Ichor Deep can be turned off.
[/list]
[/spoiler]

[size=4][b]5. Outside Interests: factions[/b][/size]

Five factions from outside Chimera want something from inside the building, and they hire you through the breaches. Your standing, jobs and enemies are tracked on [b]the Wire[/b] ([b]F9[/b]).

[spoiler]
[b]The factions[/b] and the boon each gives from Valued up:
[list]
[*][b]Kessler-Vane Holdings[/b], a rival biotech conglomerate: more damage.
[*][b]Halyard Freight Union[/b], couriers and smugglers: more loot find.
[*][b]Brassjaw Recovery[/b], bounty hunters: more crit.
[*][b]Open Ward[/b], whistleblowers freeing test subjects: more max health.
[*][b]Nullrow Exchange[/b], data brokers: more dodge.
[/list]
You start out knowing two. The others turn up as you work: as job targets, through encounters, through their people or when they come after you.

[b]Jobs:[/b] every breach shows a job board with offers from Cakewalk to Extreme. Jobs are recover, collect, clear out, take out or plant, and about half come with a bonus objective that pays even if the main job fails.

[b]Standing:[/b] reputation runs from Wanted to Allied. Climb to Trusted and then drop, and they mark you Betrayed; fall to Hated and climb back, and you're Deceitful. Favour drifts after every side area.

[b]Contacts:[/b] each faction has a contact who sells favours (kit, credits, epic gear, XP), smooths things over, finds you work or tells you what their rivals are planning.

[b]Encounters:[/b] a runner pinned down, a cell full of test subjects, a stalled extraction and more. The options depend on your standing, credits and classes.

[b]Consequences:[/b]
[list]
[*][b]Territory:[/b] a faction's operatives stand down when you're Respected and become a hit team when you're Hostile.
[*][b]Stolen goods:[/b] auction them to the owner's rivals or sell them to the fence.
[*][b]Operations:[/b] factions you've burned send ambushes and hunters, or take a cut of your credits.
[/list]

[b]Optional:[/b] it can all be turned off; breaches then work without factions.
[/spoiler]

[size=4][b]7. Crew and orders[/b][/size]

Crew is optional and off by default; turn on [b]TeamPlay[/b] in Mod Settings.
[list]
[*][b]Hiring:[/b] four crawlers can be hired in side areas through their faction: Soldier, Smuggler, Cyberninja and Force Psyker. You can have up to two at once ([b]F6[/b] opens the crew screen).
[*][b]Gear:[/b] give them weapons and armour from your pack. Their weapon replaces their basic attack, and what they wear shows on them.
[*][b]Wages:[/b] pay them every floor. Miss two paydays and they walk off with your gear, or turn on you.
[*][b]Orders:[/b] crew and Conjoined bodies take orders: Engage, Focus, Guard, Hold, Follow, Stand down, Move here, Fall back and Unleash.
[*][b]Giving orders:[/b] use the order strip ([b]O[/b]), [b]Alt+1[/b] to [b]Alt+9[/b], or right-click a companion. Your summons and pets can take orders too: turn on CommandMinions, or order one from its right-click menu.
[*][b]Idle allies:[/b] idle pets, summons and crew join fights.
[*][b]Traps:[/b] your allies don't walk onto live traps you can see, and crew and Conjoined bodies next to one try to disarm it when their odds are good.
[/list]

[size=4][b]8. Skill Synergies[/b][/size]

[b]247 pairs of skill trees[/b], vanilla and new, work together when you take both. Every synergy is a class interaction, not a flat bonus: marks spread, one class's resource pays for the other's effect, attackers get marked, and foes are shoved, stunned or silenced. Its level is the lower of the two ranks, up to 5, and raises its odds. [b]Synergy Strain[/b] sets a price on them, so a character with synergies isn't simply stronger than one without. Hover a tree on the creation screen to see what it pairs with and which pairs your picks already make. In game, the log announces new and grown synergies. A [b]Synergies[/b] view on the character screen, beside Attributes and Resistances, lists the ones you have and what each costs you, so they don't crowd your buff bar.

[size=4][b]9. Rogue Modder rebalance[/b][/size]

The Rogue Modder out-scaled every other tree: three +5 mods per item, a first mod that could never fail, and Item (Re)Designer as a free reroll. With the rebalance on (the default):
[list]
[*]Mods per item come with Modder rank: 1 at ranks 1-2, 2 at ranks 3-4, 3 at rank 5.
[*]Each mod adds 1 + rank/2 to a random stat.
[*]The 1st, 2nd and 3rd mod on an item fail 10%, 30% and 50% of the time, and a failure takes the same amount off.
[*]Item (Re)Designer keeps the mod counts, so redesign first, then mod.
[*]Gear modded before keeps its stats. Turn the option off for the vanilla rules.
[/list]

[size=4][b]13. For modders[/b][/size]

The loader is a modding framework in its own right, and the bundled mods are written with it. [i]Mods\README.md[/i] documents every field. Mods are folders of JSON or XML plus PNG, WAV and OBJ/GLB files, with a [i]mod.json[/i] for the name, version and dependencies. When data isn't enough, Lua scripts and C# plugins add to it.
[list]
[*][b]Classes:[/b] build abilities from scratch out of actions, effects, procs, conditions, counters and triggers, or borrow and tweak vanilla tiers. There's a shared library of status effects and ability templates to build on.
[*][b]Content:[/b] items, crafting recipes, creatures and summons, enemies with their own skills, synergies, factions, and side area places, kinds, anomalies and vaults.
[*][b]Assets:[/b] your own sounds, particle effects, 3D models and textures. [i]BreachworkModManager.exe --seal-audio[/i] seals a mod's sounds into an encrypted [i]sounds.pak[/i], for sound packs whose terms require embedded audio.
[*][b]Lua scripts:[/b] [i]scripts/*.lua[/i] add actions, conditions, numbers and events. They run sandboxed, with no access to files, the internet or the game's code, so they need no OK from the player.
[*][b]C# plugins:[/b] [i]plugins/*.dll[/i] can reach anything, so a mod with a plugin stays out until the player allows it. Your plugin is your own work, on your own terms.
[*][b]Portrait tools:[/b] any program can make portraits. Give it a folder with a [i]portrait_tool.json[/i] and it gets a job file with the character's classes, gear and effects. The Portrait Maker's source is included as an example.
[*][b]Patching:[/b] later mods can patch or replace earlier mods' entries by id.
[*][b]Examples:[/b] the example class, item, enemy, script and plugin mods show the format and are safe to delete. [b]Realtime with Pause[/b] is an unfinished, source-only example plugin with no dll, so it does nothing unless you build it.
[/list]
